## 3. Reading for translation to practice

1. **Darlington and C1:** nothing in PESTLE blocks the project. The binding constraints are schedule and qualification (technological and economic), so the non-technical solution is contractual (an OPG specification and a CGF/BOF tranche-2 condition), not political.
2. **Liddell:** at least four independent blocks exist: federal law, state law, the site owner, and waste. Any one is enough to stop it, so a technical improvement such as C1 does nothing for the Australian case until the legal and waste rows change. This is the honest negative finding the brief allows (R4.4).
3. **The order of the solutions matters.** Waste governance and consent rules can be built before, and independently of, any decision to repeal the bans. They are the low-regret steps. Repeal without them would repeat Kimba.
