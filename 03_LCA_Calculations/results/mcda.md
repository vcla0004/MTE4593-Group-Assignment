# MCDA: status quo vs one SMR at Liddell vs SMR + C1 vs the same money on firmed renewables

Generated by `03_LCA_Calculations/scripts/mcda.py` (analyst run 9, 21/10/2026). C1 case: **new_2200** (2,200 t CO2e/unit; switch with `--c1-case`, or `c1_cases.base` in `parameters_run9.yaml`). Raw scores per option, criterion, weight set and perturbation are in `results/mcda_scores.csv`. **PROVISIONAL:** most inputs are TO VERIFY (primaries blocked) and three criteria (supply for renewables, social licence, legal feasibility) rest on our scored judgement. The ranking is a structured comparison, not a measurement.

## 1. Method (full working)

- **Model:** linear additive (weighted-sum) MCDA, the standard compensatory model in the UK government MCA manual (Dodgson et al., 2009) [TO VERIFY: gov.uk blocked 21/10].
- **Scoring:** each criterion is min-max normalised across the four options (local scale): s = (x - worst)/(best - worst), so the best option scores 1 and the worst 0. Lower is better for life-cycle CO2e, cost, first-power year and HHI; higher is better for CO2e avoided per dollar, social licence and legal feasibility. Fixed global scales are tested in section 6.
- **Total:** T = 100 x sum_c w_c s_c, with weights renormalised to sum to 1.
- **Avoided per dollar:** cumulative CO2e avoided to 2050 for the same annual spend, each option from its own first year (same_money.md section 4 frame): spend = 2,365,200 MWh/yr x A$306/MWh = A$0.724 bn/yr; renewables buy spend / A$107 = 6.76 TWh/yr from 2028. Avoided = sum over years of MWh x (grid factor - option's life-cycle factor), grid = NEM-T2-slow (linear 640 g/kWh in 2025 to 30 in 2050). SMR 3.85 Mt, renewables 41.76 Mt to 2050; divided by A$0.724 bn/yr.
- **C1 (SMR + C1):** life-cycle reduction = saving / lifetime output = 2,200 t x 1e6 g / (2,365,200 MWh x 1,000 x 60 yr = 1.419e+11 kWh) = **0.0155 g/kWh** (0.37% of 4.2 g/kWh). C1 cost = premium x C$229/m3 x 20,000 m3 + qualification (base: 0% premium, C$1.0 m) x 1.119 AUD/CAD, annualised at CRF(7%, 30 yr) = 0.0806 over 2,365,200 MWh = **+A$0.038/MWh**. Base case: schedule-neutral (the §8.6 condition); the delay case adds the unit's carrying cost (A$ capital share x 7%/365 per day) and the CO2e not avoided during the delay.
- **Time and deliverability:** first full year of new supply. TRL is **not scored**: no TRL figure exists yet in the sections or briefs [TODO: Leroy §4.1]; schedule risk enters through the 2035 and 2045 first-power cases (section 6) and the C1 delay case.
- **Supply:** HHI of the most concentrated stage of each option's supply chain. SMR: enrichment, 2025 capacity (hhi.md). Renewables: lower bound share^2 x 10,000 from China's share of every PV manufacturing stage, > 80% (IEA, 2022) [TO VERIFY: iea.org refused 21/10]; PV stands in for the whole firmed-renewables chain (batteries and wind not assessed).
- **Social and legal:** the SMR takes its best route's total under equal weights: T13 R2/R3 (social_licence.md section 7) and App B3 route C (section 8 below). Renewables take T13 R4 and the B3 maximum (no nuclear prohibition engaged).
- **Status quo conventions (do-nothing reference):** NSW 2025 grid factor as its 'life cycle' (scope 2, a boundary mismatch, but SQ is last on this criterion whatever the boundary), nothing avoided, no new cost, no new supply chain, first year = 2050 (no new supply in the horizon), social 13 (nothing to consent to, but no benefits reach the Upper Hunter: fairness 1), legal 12. SQ cost and supply conventions are tested in section 6.

Both change the SMR's life-cycle intensity by well under 1% (0.29% and 0.37% of 4.2 g/kWh), which moves the weighted score by thousandths of a point and never changes the SMR's place against the other options. The gap is slightly negative in the base case because the assumed C$1 m qualification cost (+A$0.04/MWh) outweighs, on these scales, a 0.016 g/kWh gain on a criterion whose range is 636 g/kWh; with no qualification cost it turns positive but stays below 0.001 point. ## 9. Reading

- Ranking per weight set: **W0_equal**: VRE 80.1 > SQ 52.4 > SMR 29.7 > SMR_C1 29.7; **W1_climate_cost**: VRE 85.9 > SQ 41.7 > SMR 24.0 > SMR_C1 24.0; **W2_firm_sovereign**: VRE 63.7 > SQ 60.0 > SMR 43.6 > SMR_C1 43.6; **W3_host_community**: VRE 89.3 > SQ 55.0 > SMR 20.4 > SMR_C1 20.4.
- Firmed renewables rank first under all four weight sets. Of 56 +/-50% changes, 2 change first place (W2_firm_sovereign lifecycle x0.5 (SQ > VRE > SMR > SMR_C1); W2_firm_sovereign supply x1.5 (SQ > VRE > SMR > SMR_C1)): both in the firm-and-sovereign set, and both hand first place to the status quo, not to the SMR. No change puts an SMR option above renewables.
- The SMR beats renewables only on life-cycle CO2e (4.2 vs 30 g/kWh) and supply concentration (enrichment HHI 3,009 vs at least 6,400 for PV), and loses on CO2e avoided per dollar (10.9x), cost (2.9x), time (12 years later), social licence and legal feasibility. An SMR option ranks first only if life-cycle CO2e carries at least 94% of the weight.
- The status quo takes second place on local scales because its conventions (no new cost, no new supply chain, no legal change) score full marks on three criteria. On fixed global scales the SMR moves above the status quo under every set (section 6), so the SMR-vs-status-quo order is a scaling artefact; renewables first is not.
- SMR + C1 and SMR are indistinguishable in the MCDA under both C1 cases: C1 is worth doing only as a schedule-neutral add-on, never a reason to choose the SMR.
- Translation to practice: no single non-carbon criterion can carry an SMR to first place (section 5 thresholds: 'never' for all six), because renewables or the status quo beat it on each. An Australian SMR at Liddell therefore needs three things at once before it is competitive in this frame: a cost gap closed to near renewables, first power well before 2040, and the legal and consent barriers removed first (B3 route C; T13 R2/R3). Until then the MCDA points to the same money on firmed renewables for Australia, with SMRs kept under review against Darlington's outturn cost.
