## 1. Method

- Share of supplier *i*: s_i = 100 × q_i / Σq (percent). HHI = Σ s_i² (0 to 10,000). Equivalent number of equal suppliers N_eq = 10,000 / HHI.
- Mining is measured by country (2024 mine output, tU); conversion by company (licensed capacity, tU as UF₆ per year; production as a check); enrichment by company (capacity, thousand SWU per year, 2025 base, 2022 and 2030 as sensitivity).
- "Without Russia": Russian supply is removed and shares are renormalised over the rest, i.e. the market left to a buyer barred from Russian material, with no new capacity. "Without Russia and China": also removes CNNC, whose enrichment capacity WNA estimates on the assumption that it serves China's own fleet; this approximates the market open to a Canadian or Australian utility.
- Rows of "Others" or a residual are bounded: one supplier (upper bound) vs many small suppliers (lower bound, ~0). The difference is below 3 points in every 2024/2025 case.
- Thresholds: US DOJ and FTC (2023) *Merger Guidelines*: HHI > 1,800 is highly concentrated. The superseded 2010 *Horizontal Merger Guidelines*: < 1,500 unconcentrated, 1,500–2,500 moderately concentrated, > 2,500 highly concentrated. Both are reported because the mining result falls between them. These are antitrust screens for merger review; here they are used as a recognised scale for supply concentration, not as a legal test.

## 5. Limitations

- Capacity is not supply: nameplate SWU includes capacity Rosatom uses for underfeeding and tails re-enrichment, and CNNC's figures are WNA estimates. Secondary supply is excluded.
- Company-level enrichment concentration hides plant location: Urenco operates in the UK, Germany, the Netherlands and the USA; Orano only in France.
- Mining by country ignores ownership (e.g. Rosatom's Uranium One stakes in Kazakh mines) and transport routes for Kazakh exports [TO VERIFY]; the company-level bound partly addresses ownership.
- Merger-guideline thresholds are antitrust screens; their use here is an analogy for supply risk.

### 6.4 What the sizing means (for section 8.3)

- Darlington's whole four-unit fleet needs about **138 tSWU and 196 t U a year**: 3.20% of Urenco USA's current output capacity and 0.56% of the Russia-free, China-free enrichment market. The HHI in sections 2–4 measures the market the project buys from; the project itself is far too small to move it. The exposure is to **price and contract terms in a duopoly**, not to physical shortage.
- The two-reload reserve (51–102 t of enriched U, embodying 275–551 tSWU) is about 6.41%–12.81% of one year of Urenco USA output. It is a working-capital question (price per kg of fabricated fuel; not sourced) rather than a supply question.
- Urenco USA's announced additions (+700 tSWU by 2027; +2,100 tSWU over 2032–2036) are each larger than Darlington's four-unit demand by a factor of about 5 and 15. The binding competition is more likely to be US utilities replacing Russian SWU before the PL 118-62 waivers end on 1 January 2028 than Darlington's own volume (analyst inference).
- An Australian unit at Liddell would need the same ~34 tSWU and ~49 t U a year per unit, all of it enriched offshore (sections 2 and 5).
- All inputs are unverified or placeholders. The ratios are robust to them: even the high envelope stays at 7.00% of Urenco USA's current capacity.
