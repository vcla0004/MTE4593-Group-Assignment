# Initiative C1: cost per tonne CO2e avoided, break-even premium and carbon value (Darlington Units 2-4)

Generated by `03_LCA_Calculations/scripts/initiative_cost.py` from `parameters_run5.yaml`. **PROVISIONAL.** The LC3 saving and volumes are taken as given from `results/lca.md` (lca-analyst, 21/10; their inputs are TO VERIFY). The LC3 price premium and the qualification cost have **no source**: they are scenario values, so the result is framed as a **break-even test** (what premium the carbon price can justify), which does not need them. Reproduce: `python3 -I 03_LCA_Calculations/scripts/initiative_cost.py`.

## 1. Working

- Target concrete V = 13,081 m³ per unit (12,200-15,600); saving S = 1,730 t CO₂e per unit (1,390-2,150); n = 3 units -> **5,190 t** for Units 2-4.
- Saving per m³: s = 1,730 / 13,081 = **0.132 t CO₂e/m³** (132 kg/m³).
- GU concrete price c = C$229/m³ (municipal 32 MPa tender bids C$167-290, lead). Concrete package for the target structures: 39,243 m³ x C$229 = **C$9.0 m** for Units 2-4.
- LC3 discount scenario: production cost up to 25% lower (LC3 project claim) x binder share 40% of concrete price (assumption) = 10% cheaper concrete.
- Worked example (premium +10%, no qualification cost): A = 0.10 x 229 x 13,081 x 3 / (1,730 x 3) = 0.10 x 229 / 0.132 = **C$173/t**.

Reading: on the current path, LC3 concrete is worth up to **C$12.6–15.2/m³** more than GU concrete (C$95–115/t), about **5.5%–6.6%** of the central commodity price (4.3%–9.1% across the price range). If an Ontario LC3 supply costs more than that, or a qualification programme costs more than about C$0.49–0.60 m, the initiative costs more per tonne than the carbon price. On the superseded C$170/t the break-even would be C$22.5/m³ (9.8%).

- The whole target-concrete package is about C$9.0 m, **0.068%** of the Units 2-4 capital. Even a +20% premium (C$1.8 m) is 0.0136% of it.
- So **money is not the barrier** to C1. The barriers are qualification (who pays, and does it fit the schedule) and who captures the carbon value: construction-material emissions appear not to be covered by Ontario's EPS, which prices facility emissions [analyst inference, TO VERIFY], so no one is paid the C$/t in section 3 unless a funder or the procurement contract prices it. That is the case for a CGF/BOF milestone condition or an embodied-carbon clause in the Units 2-4 contracts (sections 8.6, 9).

## 5. Cost of one day of delay to Units 2-4, against the initiative

- Deferred output: 3 x 300 MW x 24 h x 0.9 = 19,440 MWh/day x C$149/MWh (OPG LCOE lead) = **C$2.9 m per day** (gross; output is deferred, not lost).
- Carrying cost upper bound: C$13.2 bn x 4.9% / 365 = **C$1.8 m per day** (4.9% = the real rate that reproduces OPG's C$149/MWh in lcoe.md; assumes all Units 2-4 capital is already spent).
- Carrying cost upper bound: C$13.2 bn x 7.0% / 365 = **C$2.5 m per day** (7.0% = run-1 central rate; assumes all Units 2-4 capital is already spent).
- Against: the full carbon value of C1 is C$0.49–0.60 m on the current path (C$95–115/t; C$0.88 m on the superseded C$170); a +10% premium costs C$0.90 m.
- So **one day of delay costs more than the whole initiative's carbon value, in dollars** (C$1.8-2.9 m per day against at most C$0.60 m), just as `schedule_value.md` found in tonnes (1.3-14.6 days of delay to a unit cancels its saving). The schedule-neutral condition is therefore the binding implementation requirement, in money as well as carbon: qualify the mix on mock-ups during Unit 1, before the Units 2-4 decision expected in 2028 (lead).

## 8. Gaps
- **No source for an LC3 concrete price in Ontario**, or for nuclear-grade SCC prices. The break-even (section 3) is the defensible result until one exists.
- **No source for the cost of a CSA N287.2 qualification programme.** Ask OPG or a nuclear concrete laboratory (Victor/Leroy).
- Whether calcined clay is produced at scale in Ontario (TO VERIFY): if imported, both cost and the transport emissions in the LCA change.
- Canada's industrial carbon benchmark after the 2025 review: if it is lowered or frozen, the break-even falls proportionally.
