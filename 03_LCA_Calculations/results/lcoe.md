## 1. Working

- Capital: C$20.9 bn for 4 x 300 MWe = 1,200 MWe, i.e. **C$17,417/kW** all-in (includes OPG's interest, escalation and contingency).
- Output: 4 x 300 MW x 8,760 h x 0.9 = **9.46 TWh/yr** (9,460,800 MWh).
- Opex (central, vendor-advertised BWRX-300): fixed O&M US$16.0 + variable O&M US$3.55 + fuel US$8.44 = US$27.99/MWh x 1.38 CAD/USD = **C$38.6/MWh**.
- Capital case A: K = budget. Case B: K = budget x (1 - 0.15) x F_IDC(r, 5 yr). F_IDC = 1.078 at 3%, 1.132 at 5%, 1.190 at 7%, 1.281 at 10%.
- CRF(r, n) = r(1+r)^n / ((1+r)^n - 1): 3%: 0.0361 (60 yr), 0.0510 (30 yr); 5%: 0.0528 (60 yr), 0.0651 (30 yr); 7%: 0.0712 (60 yr), 0.0806 (30 yr); 10%: 0.1003 (60 yr), 0.1061 (30 yr).
- Worked example (case B, 7%, 60 yr): K = 20.9e9 x 0.85 x 1.190 = C$21.14 bn; capital charge = K x 0.0712 / 9,460,800 MWh = C$159.1/MWh; plus opex C$38.6 = **C$198/MWh** (A$221/MWh at 1.119 AUD/CAD).

## 6. NuScale / UAMPS Carbon Free Power Project: cost-risk arithmetic

- Target price US$58/MWh (2020$) -> US$89/MWh (July 2022$). Nominal change **+53%** (the deck's '+53%').
- Restated in July 2022 dollars (CPI factor 1.14, placeholder for BLS): US$58x1.14 = US$66. Real change after subsidies **+35%** (range +32% to +37%).
- The US$89 is **net of about US$30/MWh of IRA credits** (and the DOE cost share). Before IRA credits: US$119/MWh, a real increase of **+80%** on the 2020 target.
- Project cost US$5.3 bn -> US$9.24 bn (**x1.74**): **US$20,000/kW** for 462 MWe.
- For comparison, Darlington's C$17,417/kW is about **US$12,621/kW** at 1.38 CAD/USD (different dollar years and scope: indicative only).
- DOE: US$1.355 bn award; **US$232.8 m obligated (17%)** at termination; reported net exposure about US$50 m after asset transfer. Site: Idaho National Laboratory, Idaho (UAMPS is a Utah utility group).
- Lesson for translation to practice: the project failed on **subscription** (buyers would not sign at the new price), not on licensing. Cost escalation between estimate classes (x1.74) transferred risk to municipal offtakers, who walked away. Darlington avoids this by having a single Crown owner with a regulated rate base and government equity (see the economics brief, Darlington financing).

## 8. Limitations
- Budget basis conflict (nominal with escalation vs 2024$) is unresolved; treating it as real 2024 C$ biases the LCOE **high** if escalation is included.
- No decommissioning or waste-fee line; no taxes or ITCs (the Clean Electricity ITC would lower OPG's capital cost; rate TO VERIFY).
- Opex figures are US vendor or US-fleet values; no OPG SMR opex was found.
- Constant output and no mid-life capex; first-year ramp-up ignored.
