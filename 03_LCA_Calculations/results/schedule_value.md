## 1. Working

- Monthly output of one unit: M = 2,365,200,000 kWh/yr / 12 = 197,100,000 kWh (daily 6,480,000 kWh)
- Gross loss per month of delay = M x (EF_grid(start) - EF_smr) / 10^6 t: the displacement that does not happen while the plant is late.
- Net loss per month = M x (EF_grid(start) - EF_grid(start + life)) / 10^6 t: a late plant still runs its full life, so the lost month comes back at the end of life, on a cleaner grid. Net is the honest figure for a long-lived plant; gross is the figure for 'emissions to 2050'.
- Break-even delay for an embodied-carbon saving S (t CO2e) = S / (loss per day), on both bases. Shown per 1 kt of saving so Sam's C1 result can be scaled directly. The gross basis matches carbon budgets to 2035/2050 (emissions now matter more than emissions in the 2090s); the net basis counts the whole operating life equally. A flat grid gives zero net loss by construction, which is an artefact of assuming the grid never changes.

## 6. How to use this with Sam's C1 result

Days of delay that would cancel C1 = (C1 saving per unit in kt) x (days per 1 kt, for that unit's start year and grid case). Example: if LC3 saves X kt per unit and the U2–U4 marginal-declining case gives d days per kt, then any delay longer than X x d days to a unit makes C1 net-negative for the climate. This is the test for the thesis condition 'schedule-neutral' (plan §2.4).

Caveats: (1) average factors understate what a late baseload unit's output would have displaced in Ontario, where gas is on the margin; (2) the net figure assumes the plant's life is fixed in years, not extended; (3) all inputs provisional.
