## 7 Carbon avoided: Ontario vs Liddell, the value of schedule, and the same money on firmed renewables

**For C1, schedule matters far more than concrete.** A one-month slip of Units 2–4 costs C$33–110 m in carrying cost alone, 29–175 times the carbon value of C1's whole saving, and defers about C$88 m of output. C1's 2,200 t per unit (1,100–3,300; §6.3) is cancelled by 11.6–18.5 days of delay on Ontario's average grid factor, or 1.7–1.8 days on its marginal factor. Timing also decides where an SMR helps the climate, and firmed renewables beat both sites on the same money (Appendix B4–B6).

### 7.1 The value of schedule

A late unit gives up each day's displacement (Table T10). To 2050 ("gross") the loss is permanent; over the whole life ("net") it returns at end of life, on a cleaner grid.

**In money the gap is wider.** At Canada's industrial carbon price of C$95/t in 2026 and C$115/t in 2030 (Environment and Climate Change Canada, 2026), the 6,600 t saved across Units 2–4 is worth C$0.63–0.76 m. A one-month slip of the three units costs C$33–110 m in carrying cost (3–10% a year on C$13.2 bn, an upper bound) and defers about C$88 m of output at OPG's C$149/MWh (Ontario Power Generation, 2026b). **Schedule neutrality is therefore the binding condition on C1**, and the mix must be qualified on Unit 1 mock-ups before the 2028 Units 2–4 decision (§8.6).

### 7.2 What one BWRX-300 displaces: Darlington vs Liddell

**At today's grid factors, a unit at Liddell would avoid about nine times more CO₂e than one at Darlington. Timing closes most of that gap.** One unit avoids 1.50 Mt a year on the NSW average of 640 g/kWh (DCCEEW, 2025). On Ontario's 2024 average of 73.8 g/kWh it avoids 0.165 Mt (9.1× less), and on its marginal factor of 343 g/kWh 0.80 Mt (1.9× less) (The Atmospheric Fund, 2025).

The plants would not run in the same years. Darlington Unit 1 is planned for 2030 (Ontario Power Generation, 2025a); Australia's earliest date for nuclear is 2040 (Graham, Hayward and Foster, 2024), against the Coalition's 2035 as reported (ABC News, 2024). If coal leaves the NEM by 2038 (Australian Energy Market Operator, 2024), a 2040 unit displaces only a residual 30 g/kWh (assumed).

To 2050 (Figure F6), Darlington Unit 1 avoids **1.0–6.8 Mt** and Liddell **0.67–3.9 Mt**; Liddell wins clearly only if the NEM stays at 640 g/kWh (16.5 Mt). Grid trajectory, start year and post-coal floor move these results tenfold; capacity factor and our life-cycle interval move them by a few per cent (Appendix B4). **An SMR's climate value depends on when it runs and what it displaces, not on today's coal share.**

### 7.3 The same money on firmed renewables

**At GenCost's 2030 costs, the money for one SMR unit would buy about 2.9 times as much energy from firmed renewables, which would avoid about 11 times more CO₂e to 2050 at Liddell.**

GenCost 2023-24 gives A$230–382/MWh for an SMR in 2030, against A$89–125 for solar and wind with integration costs (Graham, Hayward and Foster, 2024, as reported). With renewables from 2028 (assumed) and the SMR from 2040, renewables avoid **10.9×** more CO₂e to 2050. They still avoid 5.7× more in the worst case we tested for them (SMR A$230, renewables A$153), and 4.9× with the SMR at A$198 (Appendix B6, Table B28). If A$387–641 is the 2030 range instead, the ratios become 5.0× and 19.1×.

Renewables win because they are cheaper per MWh and start about a decade earlier; integration costs rise beyond the shares GenCost models. **On carbon per dollar, an SMR at Liddell loses.** Its case rests on firm supply, system security and a hedge against renewables falling behind, which §9 weighs.
