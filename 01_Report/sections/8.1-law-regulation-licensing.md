## 8.1 Law, regulation and licensing

Law is where Darlington and Liddell differ most. At Darlington, C1 needs only the regulator's acceptance of a concrete change in the Units 2–4 approval expected in 2028 (American Nuclear Society, 2026) [TO VERIFY]. At Liddell, three laws bar any SMR, and none can be repealed in this Parliament (Table T7).

**Australia.** Federal repeal needs 39 Senate votes. The largest plausible pro-repeal bloc (the Coalition, One Nation and Senator Babet) has 32, and Labor and the Greens (40 seats) oppose repeal (Parliament of Australia, 2026; Bowen, 2025; Australian Greens, 2025; Appendix B) [TO VERIFY]. The Coalition's pledge to "repeal all legislative prohibitions" therefore cannot pass (ABC News, 2026) [TO VERIFY]. Repeal alone would not be enough. ARPANSA, the federal radiation regulator, licenses only Commonwealth-controlled bodies, so a private plant would fall to NSW, whose own Act bans reactors (ARPANSA, n.d.) [TO VERIFY].

Of the three routes to a licensable plant [TO VERIFY]:
- a Commonwealth-owned plant under ARPANSA (the Coalition's 2024 model; ABC News, 2024) is the quickest but the easiest to reverse;
- a federal override of the state bans risks a High Court challenge (Canberra Times, 2025);
- state repeal is the most durable but the hardest to achieve.

Our scoring (Appendix B) shows the ranking depends on whether speed or durability is valued more.

**Canada.** Even at Darlington, with a willing government and a licensed site, the licence to construct one BWRX-300 took 29.1 months. It came 17.9 years after the environmental assessment began (CNSC, 2025; 2026a) [TO VERIFY]. For C1, the obstacle is qualification, not the cement standard, which already allows calcined-clay blends with up to 15% limestone (Hooton, 2023) [TO VERIFY]. Slag blends have nuclear precedent ([TODO: authors], 2026) [TO VERIFY]. We found none for calcined clay used as self-consolidating infill in the steel-plate composite modules, whose design the CNSC and US NRC jointly accepted (GE-Hitachi Nuclear Energy, 2024; US NRC and CNSC, 2025) [TO VERIFY].

**Translation to practice.** C1 stays schedule-neutral only if the mix is qualified on a full-scale mock-up during Unit 1 construction and submitted with the Units 2–4 application, with slag (S1) as the fallback (§8.6). An Australian SMR has no legal start date, so §7 treats CSIRO's 2040 "earliest" date as optimistic (Graham, Hayward and Foster, 2024) [TO VERIFY: covers SMRs?]. Building one would need every action in Table T7, and a regulator in place before sites are chosen (IAEA, 2015) [TO VERIFY edition].
