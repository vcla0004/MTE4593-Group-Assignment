## 5 Materials from extraction to end-of-life

**For C1, the binder's carbon arises in the kiln, but its risks arise in the pour and at decommissioning.** Stages follow §6.1's boundary.

**Steel.** Each unit uses 14,000 t (BWRX-300 Ostrołęka sp. z o.o., 2023). Blast-furnace steel emits about 2.33 t CO₂/t and scrap-based electric arc furnace (EAF) steel 0.68 (World Steel Association, 2026), but scrap copper embrittles vessel steel under irradiation, so nuclear plate is not a drop-in (Canadian Nuclear Safety Commission, 2023b).

**End of life.** Activation depends on trace elements: cobalt-60 and europium-152 dominate activated shield concrete (Helmholtz-Zentrum Dresden-Rossendorf, n.d.). Most decommissioning concrete is reportedly cleared, leaving "a few per cent" as waste (Thierfeldt, 2019); on an assumed 2.4 t/m³ and 2–5%, a unit's 50,000 m³ (120,000 t) leaves 2,400–6,000 t. Qualification should measure calcined clay's europium and cobalt (§10).
