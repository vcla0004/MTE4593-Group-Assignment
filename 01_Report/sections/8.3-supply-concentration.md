## 8.3 Supply concentration and geopolitics

Every processing stage at the front end of the BWRX-300 fuel chain is "highly concentrated" by the US standard, and enrichment is the worst (Table T3). The measure is the Herfindahl–Hirschman index (HHI), the sum of squared market shares (§2), and the threshold is 1,800 (US Department of Justice and Federal Trade Commission, 2023) [TO VERIFY]. For Darlington this is a price and contract risk. For an Australian SMR it is a sovereign-risk barrier on top of the bans in §8.1.

**Sanctions make it worse.** Russia holds 42.8% of world enrichment capacity (World Nuclear Association, 2025) [TO VERIFY]. Excluding Russia, as the US import ban will once its waivers end in 2028 (United States Congress, 2024) [TO VERIFY], raises the HHI at every stage. Without Russia and China, the enrichment open to Western buyers is a Urenco–Orano duopoly (HHI 5,621). No sensitivity case falls below 1,800 (Appendix B).

**Darlington and Liddell compared.** OPG's fuel chain for its first unit has no Russian stage. Its uranium comes from Saskatchewan and is converted in Ontario, enriched by Urenco USA and Orano, and fabricated into fuel in the US (Ontario Power Generation, 2023) [TO VERIFY]. Australia holds 28% of the world's identified recoverable uranium resources (below USD 130/kgU). Yet it mines only 7.6% of world output and has no conversion or enrichment plants (OECD NEA and IAEA, 2025; World Nuclear Association, 2026) [TO VERIFY]. Enrichment and fuel fabrication are among the facilities barred by EPBC s 140A and ARPANS s 10 (Parliamentary Library, 2024) [TO VERIFY]. An Australian SMR would therefore import every processed stage of its fuel, and only the Coalition proposes lifting the bans (§8.1).

**Translation to practice.** The front end is reported to be nuclear power's largest source of life-cycle emissions (United Nations Economic Commission for Europe, 2021) [TO VERIFY]. So fuel, not concrete, dominates both supply risk and footprint, and C1 addresses neither. Laser re-enrichment of depleted-uranium tails, developed by Australia's Silex Systems, could add Western supply without new mining. Yet it is being commercialised in the US, because s 140A bars enrichment plants here [TODO: source needed for Silex/GLE] (§10). Dual-source contracts with allied enrichers, like OPG's, reduce a buyer's exposure but not the HHI. The options for Australia are compared in §8.6.
