## 8.3 Supply concentration and geopolitics

Every front-end fuel stage we measured (mining by country, conversion and enrichment) is "highly concentrated". Its Herfindahl–Hirschman index (HHI, the sum of squared market shares; §2) exceeds the US threshold of 1,800 (US Department of Justice and Federal Trade Commission, 2023) [TO VERIFY]. Enrichment is the most concentrated stage (Table T3); fuel fabrication was not assessed. For Darlington this concentration is a price risk; for an Australian SMR it is a sovereign risk.

**Sanctions make it worse.** Russia holds 42.8% of 2025 enrichment capacity (calculated from World Nuclear Association, 2025) [TO VERIFY]. Removing Russia raises every HHI, and the US market must do so when the waivers on its import ban end in 2028 (United States Congress, 2024) [TO VERIFY]. Without Russia and China, enrichment is a Urenco–Orano duopoly (HHI 5,621). Of all our sensitivity cases, only mining by company falls below 1,800 (1,118; range 851–1,384, from partial data), so the mining risk is geopolitical rather than market power [TODO: Appendix B — HHI working, from results/hhi.md].

**Darlington and Liddell compared.** OPG's fuel chain for Unit 1 has no Russian stage: uranium from Saskatchewan, conversion in Ontario, enrichment by Urenco USA and Orano, and fabrication in the US (Ontario Power Generation, 2023) [TO VERIFY: primary not yet identified]. Australia holds 28% of the world's identified recoverable uranium resources (below USD 130/kgU) but mines only 7.6% of world output (OECD NEA and IAEA, 2025; World Nuclear Association, 2026) [TO VERIFY]. EPBC s 140A and ARPANS s 10 bar enrichment and fuel fabrication (Parliamentary Library, 2024) [TO VERIFY]. An Australian SMR would therefore import every processed fuel stage, and of the major parties only the Coalition would lift the bans (§8.1).

**Translation to practice.** The supply risk sits in the fuel, not the concrete, so C1 does not reduce it. Silex Systems, an Australian company, developed laser re-enrichment of depleted-uranium tails, which could add Western supply without new mining. The technology is being commercialised in the US, and s 140A would bar such a plant here [TODO: source needed for Silex/GLE] (§10). Contracts with allied enrichers, as OPG uses, are a mitigation: they reduce a buyer's exposure but leave the HHI unchanged (§8.6).
