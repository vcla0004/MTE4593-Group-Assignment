## 3 Barriers to cutting emissions with small modular reactors

**Eight barriers decide whether a small modular reactor (SMR) is built and cuts emissions; C1 (§4.3) reaches one directly and is constrained by a second.** At Darlington, Ontario, cost and schedule bind. At Liddell, the closed New South Wales coal plant we use as the Australian comparison, law comes first (§8.1–8.5).

*Table T15. Eight barriers to SMR deployment, and whether initiative C1 touches them.*
*Source: as cited in each row; working in Appendix B.*

| Barrier | Number and source | Why it matters | Does C1 touch it? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Cost and finance | **C$149–198/MWh** (Ontario Power Generation, 2026b; this study); NuScale cancelled 2023 (§8.2) | Capital is 65–86% of cost | No |
| 2. Time to deploy | **Unit 1 in service 2030**; **2040 at the earliest** in Australia (Ontario Power Generation, 2025a; Graham, Hayward and Foster, 2024) | Late plants displace less CO₂e (§7) | Yes, as a constraint |
| 3. Law | **No more than 36–37 of the 39 Senate votes** needed to repeal the federal bans (Parliament of Australia, 2026) | NSW's own ban would remain | No |
| 4. Fuel-supply concentration | **Enrichment HHI 3,009** against 1,800 (World Nuclear Association, 2025; US Department of Justice and Federal Trade Commission, 2023) | Russia holds 41–43% (§8.3) | No |
| 5. Embodied carbon and qualification | **11.5%** of life-cycle emissions is construction; C1 saves 2,200 t per unit (§6) | Infill needs nuclear qualification (§8.1) | Yes: C1's target |
| 6. Social licence | **59%** wanted the ban kept in 2024 (Griffith University Climate Action Beacon and Monash Climate Change Communication Research Hub, 2025) | No consent route (§8.4) | No |
| 7. Workforce | **AUKUS** submarine work competes for the same trades (Joint Standing Committee on Treaties, 2024) | Scarce trades (§8.4) | No |
| 8. Waste | **C$26 bn** Canadian repository, producer-funded; no Australian route (Nuclear Waste Management Organization, n.d.) | Needs a disposal route (§8.4) | No |
