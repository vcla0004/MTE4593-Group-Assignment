## 8.6 Implementation pathway

**C1 can be built without delay if OPG qualifies the mix on a Unit 1 mock-up, applies a carbon specification to non-safety concrete now, and ties a milestone to the Units 2–4 equity tranche.** The levers in Table T16 are scored on enforceability, speed and low schedule risk (1 to 3 each, our judgement from the evidence, not sourced scores). L1 and L2 score best, at 8 each. If enforceability counts double, L2 leads with 11; if speed counts double, L1 leads with 11; the recommendation (L1 now, L2 at tranche 2) is the same under both. L1 is also worth something: slag in all other plant concrete would add up to about 750 t CO₂e per unit (Table T8, S2b less S2) [TO VERIFY; not all of it is non-safety].

**Qualification and sequencing.** A CNSC licence condition is not a lever, because the CNSC regulates safety, not climate; it only has to accept the change is safe (our inference). Step 1, in 2027, is to develop slag and LC3 self-consolidating mixes (about 6 to 9 months). Step 2 is a full-scale DPSC mock-up trial off Unit 1's critical path (about 6 months), the public-funding option C in §8.2. Step 3 is the design-change evaluation, submitted with the Units 2–4 licensing package by the 2028 approval [TO VERIFY]. Steps 1 to 3 take about 18 to 24 months (our planning assumption), so they fit only if they start in 2027. If the evidence is not ready, Units 2–4 proceed with the current mix and LC3 enters at a later unit or project.

**The federal standard is a template, not an obligation.** The Standard on Embodied Carbon in Construction binds federal departments (Treasury Board of Canada Secretariat, 2022), and OPG is an Ontario Crown corporation (our inference from the scope clause) [TO VERIFY]. Its allowance of 130% of baseline for specialised mixes could let self-consolidating infill pass while emitting 30% more than the baseline. Any OPG or fund clause must therefore benchmark DPSC infill against a cement-rich self-consolidating reference mix.

**Fuel supply, which C1 does not touch (§8.3).** Table T17 compares the options for the supply risk. Dual-source contracts with allied enrichers are already used by OPG and are legal at once, but they cut exposure, not concentration. Domestic enrichment needs the federal bans repealed (§8.1).
