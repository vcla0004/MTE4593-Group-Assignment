## 1. Significant, relevant research coverage (5%)
> *HD: "Comprehensive coverage of all significant and relevant aspects of the topic. The key problems and challenges are clearly defined and discussed. Demonstrates extensive research and synthesis of a wide range of highly relevant sources."*

- [ ] **R1.1** Covers **all** significant aspects: technology and materials, fuel cycle, construction, LCA, economics, politics and law, social licence and safety, supply chain, timescales
- [ ] **R1.2** **Key problems and challenges clearly defined**: an explicit "barriers" section stating each barrier and why it matters
- [ ] **R1.3** **Synthesis**: sources compared and combined, not summarised one by one; disagreements between sources explained
- [ ] **R1.4** **Wide range of highly relevant sources**: primary sources (legislation, regulators, licensing documents, Red Book, GenCost, company filings), peer-reviewed papers, industry reports, patents. No reliance on news and blogs
- [ ] **R1.5** No irrelevant material (cut anything that doesn't serve the initiative)

## 2. Quality of technical analysis (8%)
> *HD: "High-quality technical analysis, including strong quantitative analysis and, where feasible, an eco-audit or equivalent analysis of impacts. Calculations of efficiencies, resource usage and potential environmental or economic benefits are appropriate to the scale of the proposal. Methods are clearly explained and easy to follow. Assumptions, scope and functional unit are clearly defined, where relevant. Arguments are logical, well justified and consider all important aspects. Uncertainties in results and conclusions are realistically estimated and discussed."*

- [ ] **R2.1** **Strong quantitative analysis**: real numbers, not just description
- [ ] **R2.2** **Eco-audit** (Granta EduPack Eco Audit) or equivalent LCA of impacts
- [ ] **R2.3** Calculations of **efficiencies, resource usage, and environmental and economic benefits**, **sized to the actual proposal** (our specific plant and scale, not generic nuclear)
- [ ] **R2.4** **Methods clearly explained and easy to follow**, with full working in the appendix
- [ ] **R2.5** **Assumptions, scope (system boundary) and functional unit** stated explicitly (e.g. 1 MWh delivered)
- [ ] **R2.6** Arguments logical, justified, and covering all important aspects
- [ ] **R2.7** **Uncertainty realistically estimated and discussed**: sensitivity analysis on key assumptions, ranges on results, comparison with literature values and discrepancies explained

## 3. Quality discussion of non-technical aspects (6%)
> *HD: "Comprehensive and detailed discussion of the major economic, political, social and other non-technical factors affecting translation to practice. Provides strong evaluation and comparison of solutions based on relevant non-technical objectives. Considers implementation issues and non-technical solutions where relevant, supported by evidence and quantitative/qualitative analysis where appropriate."*

- [ ] **R3.1** **Economic** factors: LCOE and capital cost risk (GenCost, the NuScale/UAMPS case), financing, market barriers to entry
- [ ] **R3.2** **Political** factors: Australian legal bans (EPBC Act s140A, ARPANS Act s10), party positions, geopolitics and supply concentration (HHI)
- [ ] **R3.3** **Social** factors: public perception vs actual safety (polling against deaths per TWh), social licence, First Nations and host-community consent, jobs
- [ ] **R3.4** **Other** factors: regulation and licensing timelines, workforce and skills, waste governance
- [ ] **R3.5** Framed as **"affecting translation to practice"**: what stops or enables our initiative being built
- [ ] **R3.6** **Evaluation and comparison of solutions against non-technical objectives** (e.g. MCDA or a scored comparison: SMR vs renewables plus storage vs the status quo)
- [ ] **R3.7** **Implementation issues and non-technical solutions** proposed (e.g. policy changes, financing models, community benefit schemes)
- [ ] **R3.8** Evidence-backed, and **quantitative or qualitative analysis** used where appropriate

## 4. Originality and creativity of the proposed solutions (3%)
> *HD: "Demonstrates a high level of originality and creativity in identifying and developing solutions that could support the chosen technology. Goes beyond obvious applications of materials engineering and identifies less intuitive or indirect opportunities. Provides new feasible ideas or connections that are not immediately apparent from the chosen technology."*

- [ ] **R4.1** The initiative goes **beyond the obvious** ("build SMRs" or "use better steel" isn't enough)
- [ ] **R4.2** At least one **less intuitive or indirect** materials-engineering opportunity identified and developed
- [ ] **R4.3** **New, feasible connections** not apparent from the technology alone (e.g. siting at retiring coal plants to reuse grid connections; displacement in Ontario vs Australia changing the climate value)
- [ ] **R4.4** The ideas are shown to be **feasible**, or clearly shown why not (the brief accepts negative findings)

## 5. Presentation (3%)
> *HD: "Professional level presentation. Writing is clear, well organised and easy to follow, with a logical flow of discussion. Good use of high-quality figures/tables where appropriate. Report is appropriately structured and concise. Consistent referencing. Very few errors in spelling, grammar or formatting."*

- [ ] **R5.1** Clear, well-organised writing with logical flow; each section leads with its point
- [ ] **R5.2** **High-quality figures and tables** where they help (LCA stage breakdown, sensitivity chart, stakeholder map, cost comparison, MCDA table), each captioned and sourced
- [ ] **R5.3** **Concise**: 15–20 pages of text, with no repetition or padding
- [ ] **R5.4** **Consistent Harvard referencing** throughout
- [ ] **R5.5** Very few spelling, grammar or formatting errors (Australian English, consistent Word styles)
