# Marking criteria and what HD looks like

The brief gives five marking criteria with no weightings or grade bands. The "HD looks like" column is our interpretation of the brief's wording, not an official rubric. Replace it if a Moodle rubric turns up.

| Criterion (brief wording) | HD looks like (our reading) |
|---|---|
| **Extent of scope**: breadth of topic; rewarded for complex topics with many aspects | Covers technical (reactor, fuel, construction materials), environmental (full LCA), economic, social and political sides, linked into one argument rather than separate essays |
| **Thoroughness of analysis**: depth and detail across technical, socio-political and economic considerations | Every major claim quantified or cited; social/political/economic factors analysed at least semi-quantitatively (e.g. HHI, cost per MWh, public opinion data); full calculations in an appendix |
| **Originality**: "tell us something we didn't know"; non-obvious materials use | A specific, non-obvious materials-engineering initiative (not just "build SMRs") with a clear finding, even a negative one |
| **Justification of methods**: realistic uncertainty, references for standard methods | Stated LCA boundary and functional unit (e.g. per kWh); assumptions table; sensitivity analysis; results compared with literature values and discrepancies explained |
| **Professional communication**: clear for a general audience | Readable by a non-specialist; consistent figures, captions and Harvard references; within 15-20 pages |

## Required content checklist (Part 2 of the brief)
- [ ] Specific initiative: named location, scale, application and technology (see brief's "better example")
- [ ] Current major barriers to reducing GHG emissions in the topic area
- [ ] Current state of the technology and how the proposal addresses the barriers
- [ ] Technical description of the improved material(s), from extraction and processing to end-of-life
- [ ] Quantitative environmental analysis, including key assumptions and sensitivity analysis
- [ ] Social, political and economic analysis (quantitative or semi-quantitative where possible)
- [ ] Triple-bottom-line evaluation of feasibility and effectiveness
- [ ] Granta EduPack LCA where feasible
- [ ] Full calculations in an appendix; results summarised in the main text
- [ ] Comparison with literature values and discussion of discrepancies
- [ ] Suggested new material properties or processes needed in future
- [ ] Independent literature search, including industry publications and patents
