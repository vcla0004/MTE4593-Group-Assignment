Novel Materials Strategies to Combat Global Warming
There is overwhelming scientific evidence that the World’s climate is rapidly changing. It is
generally accepted that human influence has been the dominant cause of the observed
global warming over the past 100 years. The extensive use of fossil fuels, land clearance
for food production and increases in affluence have combined with an uncontrolled
population increase to liberate increasing amounts of greenhouse gases into the
atmosphere.

The figures below show that although carbon dioxide is the major contributor to global
warming, other gases, such as methane (fugitive emissions from oil extraction, from
agriculture/livestock and from permafrost thawing) and nitrous oxide (from agriculture) also
play a major role. About 50 Gt/year of CO2 equivalent is currently generated by human
activities. See: https://www.epa.gov/ghgemissions/overview-greenhouse-gases and
https://www.iea.org/geco/.




There is an urgent need to implement strategies to decrease and eventually reverse the
liberation of greenhouse gases into the atmosphere. It is estimated that without new
policies to mitigate climate change we can expect an increase in global mean temperature
in 2100 of 3.7 to 4.8 °C, relative to pre- industrial levels. Importantly, the current trajectory
of global greenhouse gas emissions is inconsistent with limiting global warming to below 2
°C, relative to pre-industrial levels. The value of 2 °C is seen to be the maximum
temperature rise we can accept before there is extensive species extinction, food
insecurity and social disruption. That value requires limiting CO2 equivalent concentrations
to < 450 ppm.

Efforts to control the generation of greenhouse gases are widespread in nearly every
country. Efforts can include better management of population growth and energy,
innovations in land use and new business models. Technologies involving developing new
materials and optimal use of existing materials, have a role to play in that process. A
recent publication named “Drawdown”, outlined in the websites
(https://drawdown.org/solutions/table-of-solutions), maps, measures, models, and describes
the 100 most substantive solutions to global warming”. Nearly all have a component which
can be impacted by innovative materials design and use.

Your Task, Part 2: Report (25%)

Write a report (15 – 20 pages of text, figures and graphs not included in limit) that
contains a discussion of a proposed initiative centred on new materials engineering
technologies, that may have potential to reduce greenhouse gas emissions within your
topic area. Your proposed initiatives must be specific: you must describe the specific
proposed location, scale, application and technology that your initiative will use.


 Bad example: “We propose to replace petrochemical based plastic cutlery with
 biodegradable materials.”
 Better example: “We propose to reprocess sugar cane waste from the XYZ sugar
 cane refinery in Q-Town, Queensland, turning it into poly(cutlerene) via the novel
 processing method described in the work of A. Scientist et al (2020), and using this
 material to replace 10 tonnes of polypropylene cutlery annually”


You should include:
   ●​ Discussion of the current major barriers reducing greenhouse gas emissions
      within your topic area
   ●​ A description of the current state of your chosen technology (if it currently
      exists) and how you propose that it might be applied to your specific proposal to
      address these barriers.
   ●​ Technical description of the production and usage of the improved material(s) in
      your proposed initiative, from extraction and processing through to end-of-life.
   ●​ Quantitative analysis (i.e. calculations, even if approximate) of the likely
      environmental effects of your initiative. This should include discussion of any
      underlying assumptions on which your analysis is highly dependent, and
      sensitivity analysis of these calculations.
   ●​ Analysis (quantitative (or at least semi-quantitative) where possible) of the
      interactions of social, political and economic factors with your proposed
      initiative.
   ●​ Evaluation of the initiative’s feasibility and effectiveness in improving
      sustainability across the triple bottom line.​

Note that it is not necessary that your proposed initiative actually ends up being net
beneficial; we are not expecting you to create a world-shattering new idea within the
confines of this unit, and we do not expect you to abandon your initiatives and start
again if you discover that they have fundamental problems that can’t be solved. In
many of your assignments, the proposals will turn out to be problematic in some way;
we expect you to attempt to develop them as far as possible so that they might be
successful, or clearly show why they could not be successful. If you show clearly that
an initiative will actually be detrimental (and show how), or show that an initiative has
other problems that are not easily resolved, these are also valid and useful findings.

Your Task, Part 3: Presentation (5%)

You need to work as a group and prepare a 15 minute presentation. There will be 3-5
minutes of questions after each presentation.

Each team must submit their presentation slides (including a list of references) via
Moodle by Monday 12 October at 11:55pm). The presentations are to be made during
the Workshop/Practical Class in Week 11. Attendance is compulsory.

It is expected that team members work together on both the research and presentation
aspects of this task. Starting from Week 3, approximately 1 hour of the practical class
will be allocated for you to work together on your topic.

Generally you should:

   1.​ Conduct your own independent literature search (including industry
       publications, patents, and other literature, not just research papers) on your
       topic and proposed materials engineering initiatives, and use this information to
       outline the potential role of these initiatives in greenhouse gas reduction.
   2.​ Use the various tools discussed in weeks 1-5 of MTE4593 to analyse the likely
       effects of your initiatives over their life cycle, across the triple bottom line. In
       particular, use Granta EduPack to perform quantitative environmental life-cycle
       analysis where it is feasible and adds value to your discussion.
   3.​ Provide full calculations in an appendix, but give the final results and enough
       information that they can be understood in context within the main report.
   4.​ Compare the results of your calculations with any related values discussed in
       the literature, and discuss the sources of any discrepancies
   5.​ Suggest any new materials properties and process solutions that may need to
       be developed or could assist in the future in supporting the technology for your
       chosen option.

Marking:

In marking your work, we will consider:

    ●​ Extent of scope: breadth of the chosen topic, and how much information is
       available. Teams will be rewarded for attacking topics that are complex and
       comprise many different aspects.
    ●​ Thoroughness of analysis: depth and attention to detail, how well you consider
       all the individual parts that fall within that scope, including technical,
       socio-political and economic considerations.
    ●​ Originality: tell us something we didn’t know. Try to look at some of the less
       intuitive solutions in the Drawdown site, not just solutions obviously directly
       related to materials use, and/or you could propose some non-obvious use of
       materials in a particular solution scenario.
    ●​ Justification of methods: this should include a realistic assessment of
       uncertainties in your conclusions, and appropriate references where standard
       methods are used.
    ●​ Professional communication: information clearly explained for a general
       audience.
