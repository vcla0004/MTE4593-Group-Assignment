Leroy (about 1.5 minutes). This is our specific proposal. Darlington’s first unit already has its concrete in, so we target Units 2 to 4: three more 300 MWe reactors at the same site in Clarington, Ontario. The application is the concrete that fills the steel-composite modules of the reactor building, and the deep shafts for cooling water. The technology is LC3, limestone calcined clay cement (Scrivener et al., 2018), where calcined clay and limestone replace part of the clinker, the high-emission part of cement. The hard part: the concrete has to flow between steel plates, stay cool in thick sections and qualify under Canada’s nuclear concrete standards.
