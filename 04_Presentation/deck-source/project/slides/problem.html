Coal is closing. What replaces its firm power?

640 g

CO₂e per kWh from the NSW grid today, still mostly coal

5–6 g

CO₂e per kWh over a nuclear plant’s whole life cycle

Retiring coal leaves a gap for reliable, low-carbon power. SMRs are pitched as one way to fill it.

Sources: DCCEEW (2025) National Greenhouse Accounts Factors; UNECE (2021) Life Cycle Assessment of Electricity Generation Options

Leroy (about 1 minute). Start with the contrast: every kWh from the NSW grid emits about 640 grams of CO₂e at the power station, because it is still mostly coal. A nuclear plant emits about 5 to 6 grams per kWh across its whole life, from mining to decommissioning (UNECE 2021). As coal plants like Liddell close, Australia needs reliable low-carbon power to replace them. That is the gap small modular reactors claim to fill. Transition: “So what is an SMR?”
