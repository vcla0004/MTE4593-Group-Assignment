Darlington proves the technology. Its climate payoff is far larger on a coal-heavy grid like Australia’s.

Our calculation; grid factors: DCCEEW (2025), Ontario Government (2024); nuclear 6 g/kWh: UNECE (2021). Assumes 90% capacity factor

Victor (about 1 minute). This is our own calculation. One 300 MWe unit at a 90% capacity factor makes 300 × 8,760 × 0.9 = 2.37 TWh a year. In NSW each kWh replaces about 640 g of grid emissions and adds about 6 g of its own life-cycle emissions, saving about 634 g per kWh, or 1.5 million tonnes of CO₂e a year. In Ontario the grid is already about 53 g per kWh (mostly hydro and nuclear), so the saving is only about 47 g per kWh, or 0.11 million tonnes. That is about 13 times less. Caveats: this uses average grid factors, not the exact plant being displaced, and the capacity factor is our assumption. Our point: Darlington proves the technology, but the climate case is much stronger in Australia. So why aren’t we building one?
