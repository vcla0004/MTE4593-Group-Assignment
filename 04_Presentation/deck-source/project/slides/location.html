Victor (about 1 minute). This is our own calculation. One 300 MWe unit at a 90% capacity factor makes 300 × 8,760 × 0.9 = 2.37 TWh a year. In NSW each kWh replaces about 640 g of grid emissions and adds about 6 g of its own life-cycle emissions, saving about 634 g per kWh, or 1.5 million tonnes of CO₂e a year. Ontario’s grid is already clean, mostly hydro and nuclear, at 35 to 74 g per kWh depending on the year and source. So the saving there is 0.07 to 0.16 million tonnes, about 9 to 22 times less. Two honest caveats. First, if you count the gas plants an SMR would actually push off Ontario’s grid (the marginal factor), Ontario saves 0.8 to 1.1 Mt and the gap shrinks to about 1.4–1.9 times. Second, timing: Australia couldn’t have an SMR running before about 2040, and by then the grid will be much cleaner. Avoided emissions to 2050 at Liddell range from 0.6 Mt (if coal is gone by 2038) to 3.8 Mt on a slower transition. So the location matters, but so does speed.
