Victor or Leroy, covering Sam’s part (about 1.5 minutes). Bar lengths are to scale. Coal is 751 to 1,095 grams of CO₂e per kWh over its life cycle, and gas combined cycle 403 to 513 grams. Nuclear is 5.1 to 6.4 grams, so its bar is almost invisible (UNECE 2021). Our own model gives a median of 4.2 g per kWh (90% range 3.0 to 5.8), just below UNECE’s 5.1–6.4 range, though our 90% range overlaps it. The largest share is uranium mining and milling (about 29%), then conversion, whose energy use is very uncertain. Switching the targeted concrete to LC3 saves about 1,700 tonnes of CO₂e per unit (1,400 to 2,150), or about 5,200 tonnes for Units 2 to 4, which is only about 0.3% of a unit’s life cycle. Two honest findings: a slag blend saves almost as much as LC3, so slag is our fallback; and switching all of the plant’s steel to a lower-carbon route would save more again, though within the same target structures LC3 saves more than steel would. Construction is a small share of nuclear’s emissions. That is an honest finding, and it’s why our main climate argument is about what the reactor replaces and how fast it is built.
