The BWRX-300: a smaller, simpler boiling water reactor

300 MWe

About a fifth of a large reactor, built in a factory-style series

Passive cooling

Natural circulation and isolation condensers, with no pumps needed to cool the core

>50% less concrete

Per MW, versus GE Hitachi’s large ESBWR design (vendor claim)

Source: GE Hitachi Nuclear Energy (2021) BWRX-300 presentation, Alberta Innovates SMR Learning Series

Leroy (about 1 minute). The BWRX-300 is GE Vernova Hitachi’s 300-megawatt boiling water reactor. Three points: it is small, about a fifth the size of a large reactor; it cools itself passively, with water circulating naturally and isolation condensers, so no pumps are needed to remove heat after shutdown; and GE Hitachi claims it uses more than half as much concrete and building volume per MW as its large ESBWR. Stress that this is the vendor’s claim, compared with their own large design, and nobody has measured it yet. Transition: “The first real test of those claims is being built right now in Canada.”
