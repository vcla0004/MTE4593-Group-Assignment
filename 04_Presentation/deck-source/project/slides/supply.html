Victor (about 1 minute). Fourth barrier: fuel supply. Australia holds about 28% of the world’s identified recoverable uranium, the largest share of any country (NEA/IAEA Red Book 2024). But we mine it and ship it as ore concentrate: there is no conversion or enrichment here. Russia holds about 43% of global enrichment capacity (42.8% in 2025, World Nuclear Association). The Herfindahl-Hirschman index (HHI) adds up the squared market shares; the US DOJ/FTC 2023 Merger Guidelines treat anything above 1,800 as highly concentrated. Enrichment comes out at about 3,000, 3,600 without Russia, and about 5,600 once China is also excluded, when it is essentially just Urenco and Orano. Uranium mining (2,341) and conversion (2,111) are also highly concentrated. That is a geopolitical risk for any reactor here. Mitigation (it reduces a buyer’s exposure, not the HHI): long-term contracts with allied enrichers such as Urenco and Orano, and over time, possibly domestic conversion.
