Barrier 1 · Law

Nuclear power is illegal in Australia today

EPBC Act 1999, s140A

The Environment Minister must not approve a nuclear power plant

ARPANS Act 1998, s10

The national radiation regulator must not licence one

What would have to change: Parliament repeals both bans, then builds a licensing regime from scratch. Canada’s took 2.5 years for one licence.

Sources: Environment Protection and Biodiversity Conservation Act 1999 (Cth); Australian Radiation Protection and Nuclear Safety Act 1998 (Cth); NucNet (2025)

Victor (about 1 minute). The first barrier is legal. Two federal laws ban nuclear power. Section 140A of the EPBC Act stops the Environment Minister approving a nuclear power plant, and section 10 of the ARPANS Act stops ARPANSA, the national radiation regulator, from licensing one. So our proposal needs Parliament to repeal both bans, then build a licensing system. Even in Canada, with an experienced regulator, OPG’s construction licence took two and a half years (October 2022 to April 2025). This is a non-technical barrier, and it has a non-technical solution: legislation plus a well-resourced independent regulator. It needs political will that isn’t there yet. Transition: “Even if the law changed, there’s the cost.”
