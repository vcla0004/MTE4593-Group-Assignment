Barrier 1 · Law

Nuclear power is banned in Australia today

EPBC Act 1999, s140A

The Environment Minister must not approve a nuclear power plant

ARPANS Act 1998, s10

The national radiation regulator must not license one

NSW Act 1986, s8

Bans nuclear power reactors in NSW, including at Liddell

What would have to change: federal and NSW repeal, and a regulator able to license private operators. Canada’s construction licence alone took 29 months.

Sources: EPBC Act 1999 (Cth); ARPANS Act 1998 (Cth); NSW Prohibitions Act 1986; CNSC (2025)

Victor (about 1 minute). The first barrier is legal. Three laws stand in the way at Liddell. Section 140A of the federal EPBC Act stops the Environment Minister approving a nuclear power plant. Section 10 of the ARPANS Act stops ARPANSA, the national radiation regulator, from licensing one. And NSW’s own 1986 Act bans nuclear power reactors in the state (research reactors like Lucas Heights are exempt). The site owner, AGL, has also ruled out nuclear at Liddell. Even after repeal there’s a gap: ARPANSA only regulates Commonwealth entities, so a private operator would need a new licensing role. Politics: repeal needs a Senate majority, and both Labor and the Greens oppose it. A NSW repeal bill passed the upper house in May 2026 but has stalled in the lower house. In Canada, with an experienced regulator, OPG’s construction licence alone took 29 months. The non-technical solution is legislation plus a capable independent regulator, which needs political will that isn’t there yet.
