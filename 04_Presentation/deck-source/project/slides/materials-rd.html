Sam (about 1 minute). Three things materials engineering still has to solve. First, our own proposal: an LC3 mix that flows between the steel plates of the modules, doesn’t overheat in thick pours, and qualifies under Canada’s CSA N287 nuclear concrete standards. That is the main feasibility risk of our idea. Second, the bigger hotspot: the fuel chain’s front end. SILEX, an Australian-invented laser enrichment technology, is being commercialised in the US to re-enrich leftover depleted-uranium tails. It can’t be built here because of the nuclear ban. Third, waste: a 2022 PNAS study estimated SMRs could produce 2 to 30 times more waste by volume per unit of energy. NuScale disputes this, so present it as contested.
