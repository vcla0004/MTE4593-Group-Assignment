Source: Krall, Macfarlane and Ewing (2022) Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences 119(23)

Sam (about 1 minute). Three things materials engineering still has to solve. First, our own proposal: reclaimed ash must be proven consistent and clean enough for nuclear-grade concrete, and durable under heat and radiation for 60+ years. That is the main feasibility risk of our idea. Second, accident-tolerant fuel cladding, such as coated zirconium alloys or silicon carbide composites, which hold up better if the core overheats and produce less hydrogen. Third, waste: a 2022 PNAS study (Krall, Macfarlane and Ewing) estimated SMRs could produce 2 to 30 times more waste by volume per unit of energy, mainly from neutron reflectors and other core materials. NuScale disputes this, so present it as a contested finding. Handover: “Victor will show why where you build it matters even more than how.”
